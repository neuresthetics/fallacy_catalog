## Conclusion first (what corporate use looks like today)

- **MINDSPACE by name is niche in corporate speech.** The 2010 Cabinet Office / Institute for Government checklist still appears in consultancy playbooks (e.g. Umbrex change-management), marketing-agency blogs, gamification/behavioural-design guides, and some clinical/health-IT research. Most private-sector work **does the same levers without saying "MINDSPACE."** *(Second pass sample of Fortune careers/annual-report language found near-zero “MINDSPACE” hits; “behavioural science / nudge / choice architecture / EAST” appear more often in consulting and careers copy than in 10-Ks.)*
- **What companies actually say:** nudge, choice architecture, behavioural design / behavioural science, growth, UX conversion, dark patterns / sludge (critics), EAST (BIT's simpler successor), COM-B, Fogg, "persuasion," "engagement," online choice architecture (CMA).
- **Corporate uptake is real and institutionalised.** McKinsey (2019) documented in-house "nudge units" at insurers, banks, retailers, and utilities; named practitioners at Walmart, Google (via Irrational Labs alumni), Lemonade, Betterment, Prudential, Commonwealth Bank, Swiss Re, Morningstar, Bupa, Maritz, Ogilvy. Deloitte and BCG sell behaviour-change / behavioural-economics consulting. BIT itself now consults private clients (e.g. unnamed global biopharma safety culture, 2025; Capita/Level payroll savings; Virgin Atlantic flight-ops field experiment published via HBR).
- **Strongest documented corporate levers:** **Defaults** (401(k) / UK auto-enrolment; subscription auto-renew; cookie/consent banners; CRM workflow defaults), **Norms** (social proof, energy comparisons, "people like you"; Mathur et al. found social-proof activity messages as a major shopping-site category, often via third-party SaaS), **Messenger** (influencers, peer champions, C-suite vs peer), **Incentives** (loyalty, loss-framed offers), **Affect** (fear/urgency in marketing; care framing in safety), **Commitments** (public pledges, Save More Tomorrow), **Salience** (checkout buttons, deadlines, dashboards, countdown timers). **Priming** remains the weakest and most contested for commercial scale (replication crisis); **Ego** shows up as brand/identity more than as a named lever.
- **Same toolkit, opposite ethics.** Pension auto-enrolment and Amazon Prime "Iliad" cancellation friction both rest on Defaults + friction design. Regulators now treat covert commercial choice architecture as **dark patterns / deceptive design / OCA** (FTC, CMA Behavioural Hub, EU DSA/DMA, OECD, UOKiK Amazon countdown case), not as clever UX. Brignull types map cleanly onto MINDSPACE levers (see second-pass table).
- **For detection work:** assume corporate copy and product flows are lever-rich even when they never cite MINDSPACE. Score levers separately from fallacy validity; "behavioural science" branding does not imply transparency. Prefer Norms/Defaults claims with field RCTs; discount Priming and unverified urgency/scarcity copy.
- **Gap (narrowed but not closed):** few public Fortune-500 case studies that say "we used MINDSPACE levers X/Y." Stronger evidence for *equivalent* practice under other names, consulting/agency MINDSPACE teaching content, regulator taxonomies, and peer-reviewed dark-pattern crawls. Second pass adds sector cases, Brignull↔MINDSPACE map, HR/safety citations, ethics/sludge literature, and replication caveats.

### Composite vignette (named framework, anonymised company)

Umbrex's change-management write-up describes a **~$900M industrial distributor** CRM rollout: training high, usage low. Mapped with MINDSPACE: regional leaders as messengers; spreadsheet as de facto default; veteran bypass as norm; buried fields (salience); ego threat from looking less effective. Interventions: forecast meetings only accept CRM data (default), public leader use, team adoption dashboards (norms), UI simplify, top-rep wins stories. Claimed material usage lift within a quarter. *(Consulting vignette — treat as illustrative, not independently audited.)*

### Clinical / enterprise IT (named MINDSPACE)

Monash / Australian Digital Health Agency systematic review (Int J Med Inform, 2023): MINDSPACE used to analyse clinical decision-support **alerts and reminders**; defaults and norms most used; ~71% of reviewed designs informed by MINDSPACE effects reported success influencing clinical decisions. Shows the acronym travelling into **healthcare product/UX** design, not only marketing.

## Ethics, regulation, and criticism

1. **Publicity / revelation test** (Thaler–Sunstein / BIT practitioners in McKinsey interviews): would you publish what behaviour you're targeting, which levers, who pays, and who gains/loses? Interventions that *require opacity* fail the test — McKinsey quotes practitioners using this standard.
2. **MINDSPACE report itself** flagged transparency and public consent as design requirements; Decision Lab restates that full transparency is needed for autonomy.
3. **Dark patterns enforcement (US):** FTC (Jun 2023) sued Amazon for dark-pattern Prime enrollment and deliberately hard cancellation ("Iliad" flow); alleged ROSCA and FTC Act violations. Later reporting describes a large settlement and "click to cancel"-style obligations industry-wide. Concrete map: **Defaults + Salience (asymmetric CTAs) + obstruction (anti-Cancel)** = commercial MINDSPACE-without-the-name.
4. **EU:** DSA Article language on manipulative interfaces; DMA anti-circumvention and consent/default rules for gatekeepers; AI Act restrictions on subliminal/manipulative techniques; 2022 Commission dark-patterns study cited ~**97%** of popular EU consumer websites/apps using at least one dark pattern; Digital Fairness fitness check (2024) and proposed further action (Digital Fairness Act track into 2025).
5. **OECD (2022)** *Dark commercial patterns*: taxonomy (forced action, interface interference, nagging, obstruction, sneaking, social proof, urgency) explicitly ties commercial UI to behavioural biases (default bias, scarcity, social proof, framing).
6. **Academic / critic lines:** libertarian paternalism critiques (infantilisation, value imposition); priming durability doubts; "nudge isn't enough" social-identity critiques; fear-nudge ethics (COVID-era). Corporate side often quieter than government about admitting nudging because of "dark side" optics (McKinsey observation).
7. **Detection implication:** corporate denial of "MINDSPACE" or "nudge" is not evidence of non-use. Look for lever mechanics in the interface and copy.

## Gaps / weakly evidenced claims

- **Few public Fortune-500 write-ups that say "we applied MINDSPACE."** Stronger evidence for *equivalent* practice under nudge/behavioural-design branding.
- **Umbrex distributor vignette** and many agency blogs are **illustrative / marketing**, not peer-reviewed RCTs.
- **Priming** corporate claims should be discounted pending local experiments.
- **Effect sizes and persistence** of campaign-style levers (salience, affect) often decay when the intervention stops; architectural defaults stick longer (Allcott & Rogers energy-bill follow-ups are the cleanest long-horizon public evidence — utility sector, not pure "corporate marketing").
- **"Mindspace" brand collision:** several unrelated agencies named Mindspace (gamification, Dubai marketing) — not the 2010 framework.
- **This brief does not audit** Jason's GitHub AutoNudge-era experiments; those remain out of scope except as "past learning" per brief.

## Practical detection cheat-sheet (corporate text / UX)

When scanning corporate material, ask:

1. Who is speaking, and is sponsorship hidden? → **Messenger**
2. Is the offer loss-framed, time-pressured, or loyalty-bundled? → **Incentives**
3. Are peers/neighbours/"most customers" invoked? → **Norms**
4. What happens if the user does nothing? Is cancel harder than join? → **Defaults** (+ sludge)
5. What is visually loud vs buried? → **Salience**
6. Environmental/onboarding cues without content change? → **Priming** (flag, low confidence)
7. Fear, warm glow, outrage, urgency emotion? → **Affect**
8. Public pledge, checkbox identity, "you'll follow through"? → **Commitments**
9. Identity/brand flattery ("smart," "responsible," "elite")? → **Ego**

Keep fallacy validity in a separate column — a HOLD claim can still be lever-heavy; a FAIL claim dressed in Norms/Affect is camouflage.

### 5. Critiques — libertarian paternalism, sludge, manipulation vs persuasion

1. **Libertarian paternalism pushback:** classic line (e.g. from political theory and business-ethics commentary) that choice architects impose values while claiming neutrality; “governance by stealth.” Mols et al. (*European Journal of Political Research*) — “Why a nudge is not enough: a social identity critique of governance by stealth” — argue identity and group processes outperform stealth micro-nudges for durable change; DOI resolves via Cambridge (verified redirect chain).
2. **Sludge (Sunstein and followers):** *friction that impedes*, often asymmetrically (easy join / hard leave). Corporate “optimisation” of cancel flows is sludge by design; FTC/CMA cases are sludge enforcement under consumer-law labels.
3. **Manipulation vs persuasion (business ethics):** persuasion is typically characterised as open reasons-giving; manipulation as bypassing or subverting deliberative capacity (hidden Defaults, fake Norms, Affect under false pretences). The MINDSPACE report’s own transparency / public-consent requirements and Thaler–Sunstein **publicity test** (restated by McKinsey interviewees) are the practical bright line — interventions that *only work if undisclosed* fail it.
4. **Corporate quietness:** McKinsey (2019) notes firms under-advertise nudge units because of “dark side” optics — consistent with detection stance: look at interfaces, not press releases.
5. **Digital Fairness / DSA framing:** EU materials treat certain patterns as unfair commercial practices regardless of whether the firm hired a “behavioural scientist.”

### 6. Replication / effect-size caveats (commercial claims)

| Lever / claim class | What holds up better | What to discount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Defaults** | Large, replicated participation effects (Madrian & Shea 401(k); UK auto-enrolment literature). Sticky when architecture stays. | Claims that *any* pre-tick is “for the user’s good” without opt-out symmetry. |
| **Norms** | Opower-style neighbour comparisons: ~2% energy effect; Allcott & Rogers show **multi-year persistence with decay**. Mathur shows Norms/social-proof UI is industrially common — but **prevalence ≠ truthful norms**. | Fake activity messages, purchased testimonials, “X people viewing” widgets from third-party SaaS with no audit trail. |
| **Priming** | Environmental cues can matter in narrow lab/retail contexts. | **Social-priming replication crisis** — Umbrex and Chou (first pass) already warn low confidence. Do not treat commercial “prime them with words on the splash screen” claims as established. |
| **Salience / urgency / scarcity** | Real when the constraint is real (true stock, true deadline). | Mathur: many timers/stock messages are **non-binding or always-on** → Affect theatre, not information. Regulators (UOKiK) treat false urgency as dark patterns. |
| **Affect / fear** | Can move short-run compliance (safety reframes; charity appeals). | Habituation; ethical and legal limits (AI Act / DSA language on manipulative techniques); COVID-era fear-nudge debates. |
| **Messenger** | Peer vs authority effects are real in field work. | Undisclosed sponsored messengers (influencer marketing without #ad) — ethics and ad-law issue. |
| **Incentives** | Price and loss-framing work — but are often just pricing, not “behavioural magic.” | Overclaiming tiny A/B lifts as durable behaviour change. |
| **General commercial A/B lore** | Prefer peer-reviewed field experiments and regulator evidence reviews (CMA 261-page OCA evidence paper) over agency blog absolutes. | Single-site CRO case studies without holdout / without pre-registration. |

**Practical rule for this bot’s detection work:** score the lever; separately score **evidence grade** (RCT / regulator finding / consultancy vignette / blog). Norms and Defaults earn higher prior confidence than Priming and unverified Urgency.

### 9. Critiques — expanded

1. **Libertarian paternalism:** value imposition under neutrality claim; soft compulsion via Defaults.
2. **Governance by stealth / social identity (Mols et al.):** durable change needs identity & groups, not only micro-stealth; opaque nudges can backfire politically inside firms too.
3. **Sludge audits (Sunstein, *Behavioural Public Policy*):** organisations should inventory frictions; cancel/benefits/appeals are prime sludge sites — same toolkit as “good” Easy nudges, inverted.
4. **Manipulation vs persuasion:** reasons-giving vs bypassing deliberation; fake Norms and accidental-purchase Defaults fail even weak persuasion tests.
5. **Publicity test:** if disclosure kills effectiveness *and* harms the chooser’s goals, suspect manipulation; if disclosure preserves effect, closer to legitimate choice architecture (nudge+ literature).
6. **Nudgeability:** effects strongest when aligned with latent preferences — limits fantasies of total preference override; also warns against claiming “we only nudge what’s good for them” without evidence of preference alignment.
7. **Corporate optics:** McKinsey — firms stay quiet about nudge units → detection must be interface-first.

### 12. Detection playbook for mindspace bot

When the acronym is absent, still score levers. Practical stack:

1. **Capture the artefact** — screenshot, HTML CTA hierarchy, email copy, careers JD, policy PDF.
2. **Run the nine questions** (first-pass cheat-sheet): Messenger / Incentives / Norms / Defaults / Salience / Priming / Affect / Commitments / Ego.
3. **Stack detection** — commercial harm rarely uses one lever. Classic dark stack: **Defaults + Salience asymmetry + fake Norms/Urgency + Affect**. Classic “good” savings stack: **Defaults + Commitments (+ optional Norms)**. Classic safety stack: **Messenger + Affect + Norms + Timely Salience (EAST)**.
4. **Publicity-test gate** — Would the firm publish the targeted behaviour, lever, payer, and winner/loser? Fail → flag manipulation risk even if legal grey.
5. **Truth test for Norms/Urgency/Scarcity** — Is the claim falsifiable and currently true? If always-on or unverifiable → treat as deceptive Norms/Salience, not healthy social information.
6. **Symmetry test for Defaults** — Is exit as easy as entry (Click-to-Cancel / CNIL reject-as-easy)? Asymmetry = sludge.
7. **Evidence grade tag** — `RCT` / `regulator` / `vendor-case` / `blog` / `anecdote`. Never upgrade a blog to RCT in memory.
8. **Framework alias map** — If copy says EAST, COM-B, Fogg, CRO, OCA, dark patterns, sludge — translate into MINDSPACE levers for a common detection schema; keep original label in notes.
9. **Separate fallacy validity** — A HOLD factual claim can still be Norms/Affect-loaded; a FAIL claim dressed in social proof is camouflage.
10. **Seniority of source for actors** — Prefer McKinsey-named roles, BIT/primary regulators, peer-reviewed field experiments over agency SEO posts when writing conclusions.
11. **Priming rule** — Log possible priming but **do not** treat as high-confidence corporate strategy evidence without local experiment.
12. **Output fields for bot** — `{levers[], stack_name, publicity_pass, truth_pass, symmetry_pass, evidence_grade, aliases[], urls[]}`.

### 2. AdTech / recommender / personalization as Salience + Messenger + Affect

- **Retail media networks** (Amazon Ads, Walmart Connect, Instacart Ads / Carrot Ads) industrialise first-party behavioural targeting: browse/purchase/cart/loyalty signals rank which product, offer, or creative appears at the moment of choice — continuous **Salience** allocation plus paid **Messenger** (brand creative) and **Affect** (deals, seasonal urgency).
- **Concrete case — Instacart Caper Carts (8 Jan 2024 press):** AI smart-cart screens show (a) home-screen banners for new products/deals/seasonal promotions and (b) **personalized complementary recommendations from real-time cart contents** (e.g. cones → Dreyer’s ice cream). Launch partners Del Monte, Dreyer’s, General Mills. Extends online retail-media logic into the physical aisle — Norms-lite (“people buying X also…”) without needing to say “nudge.” [#86]
- **Responsible-recommendation literature** (AI & Ethics strand) argues platforms should expose controls/opt-outs/explanations; corporate practice optimises conversion/ROAS first.
- **Lever stack (detection):** ranked feed = Salience; sponsored label opacity = Messenger confusion; FOMO creatives = Affect; “customers also bought” = Norms; one-click add / pre-checked add-ons = Defaults.

### 4. Workplace surveillance + “wellness” as Ego / Affect / Norms

- **Mechanism:** employer wellness platforms (e.g. Virgin Pulse-class products) ship **challenges, leaderboards, social feeds, wearable sync, rewards** — descriptive **Norms** (team standings), **Ego** (“be the kind of employee who…”, badges), **Affect** (streak anxiety, loss of points), sometimes **Incentives** (premium discounts).
- **Consent problem:** Arawjo et al. arXiv 2023 — workers rarely meet FRIES (freely given, reversible, informed, enthusiastic, specific) for workplace wellbeing sensing; structural power makes “opt-in” thin. [#93]
- **Privacy / discrimination:** Mateu & Mäihäniemi *IDPL* 2020 — mHealth wearables share sleep/activity/condition signals with employers/insurers; GDPR tensions; non-discrimination risk. [#94]
- **Nudge acceptability:** Spiekermann et al. *JSIS* 2023 Q-methodology — employees accept some wearables nudges (fun/positive reinforcement, self-responsibility) more than controlling environments; demand transparent dialogue. [#95]
- **Harms cascade:** ACM FAccT systematic review of biometric workplace monitoring — emotional labour to game affect sensors; productivity inferences from “wellness” data.
- **Detection for mindspace bot (employee side):** leaderboard / challenge copy → Norms+Ego; “your peers completed…” → Norms; opt-out buried or premium-tied → Defaults/sludge; mood/productivity AI → Affect surveillance.
